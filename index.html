    Milestones

    Before you paste anything: data, privacy and work rules▾

    Most of this playbook uses consumer AI tools. They are safe for most of your work if you follow a few habits.

    Never paste

    • Ad account access tokens, passwords, or anything from Business Manager settings
    • Lead data with names, phone numbers or emails (lead forms, CRM exports). Strip the personal columns first
    • Client contracts or unreleased financials, unless your company has approved a business-tier AI tool for that

    Do this once, today

    • ChatGPT: Settings → Data controls → turn off “Improve the model for everyone”. Claude: Settings → Privacy → review the training setting. Gemini: Activity settings.
    • Ask your manager or IT what the company position on AI tools is. If none exists, say you are using them for drafting and analysis and that you anonymise client names (“Client A”).
    • Meta may add an AI info label to ads made or materially edited with its generative features. Tell clients before you turn those features on.
    • Free meeting-recorder tiers sometimes use your audio to train models. Check the privacy page before recording a client call, and tell attendees you are recording.
    • Any automation that sends something to a client gets a human approval step. Always.
    • AI gets numbers wrong with total confidence. Check three figures against the source every time.

    Copy and use

    Prompt library

    Eleven prompts written for your job. Replace anything in [square brackets]. Save the ones you use weekly as a Claude Project, a Custom GPT, or a Gemini Gem so they become one click.

    The four-part prompt (the only prompting theory you need)

    1. Role: who the AI is (“You are a senior Meta Ads strategist”).
    2. Context: client, audience, goal, what has happened so far. Paste real material.
    3. Task: one clear verb. Draft, summarise, compare, list, rewrite.
    4. Format: length, structure, tone, what to leave out. “Three bullets, under 60 words each, no exclamation marks.”

    Then iterate: “shorter”, “more formal”, “give me five more angles”, “which of these would you cut and why”. The second message is where the quality comes from.
